An empty evening is a problem a small venue knows well. You have a room, a budget, and people who could make something happen. What you do not have is a reason for strangers to choose it. ASSEMBLY starts with that problem. This is a fictional rehearsal at The Fold.

We give the evening a clear ambition: shared listening, conversation, and food. The room has forty-eight places. We review the budget, the sound limit, and the actual offers entered for this demonstration. Those constraints matter before anyone writes an attractive invitation.

Next, we choose the exact cultural identities behind the brief. A name can be ambiguous. Qloo supplies connections across artists, films, and books; the operator confirms which identities belong here. Cultural affinity is a starting point for an idea, not evidence that a local performer is available.

The AI producer inspects the inventory, consults those references, and composes three possible nights. The application checks the entered offers and costs. We inspect the evidence, then choose shared listening with Nila and The Low Lights, followed by Little Table. A SAULT reference helps set the atmosphere.

The research runs in a private worker on Google Cloud. Cloud Tasks delivers the job, while PostgreSQL saves its progress. The operator can change screens without bringing provider credentials into the browser.

Now the proposal becomes a specific promise. The host reviews the date, program, free admission and room limits before publishing. Morgan, our sample guest, enters a name and chooses a party of two. That first response records interest; it does not hold a seat. Morgan then confirms the choice on the exact current terms. Separately, the supplier reviews the date, fee, setup and performance slots before confirming the assignment. Guest interest, supplier availability and host approval are different decisions, made in different views.

Music and media permissions, access and dietary needs, and staffing and safety each need their own check.

Once the production checks and host approval are current, free sample reservations can open. Morgan reserves two places. Then the music supplier withdraws.

Bookings pause immediately. The existing places stay held. We propose Sunday People as the replacement and review the changed program and higher cost. The replacement must confirm its own assignment before the host can approve the change.

But replacing a supplier does not replace the guest's decision. Morgan sees the revised terms and chooses again. The host renews the production responsibilities. Only then can this production resume.

The same desk carries the updated run of show, task evidence, readiness, and check-in. We finish the rehearsal with two sample arrivals and no money collected.

That demonstrates the software's connected workflow. It does not establish real demand, supplier participation, performance rights, or event success. Paid ticketing and email delivery remain outside this release. ASSEMBLY helps a cultural possibility become a plan people can choose, and reconsider when the promise changes.

Cultural evidence supports decisions. Suppliers and audiences confirm their own choices.
